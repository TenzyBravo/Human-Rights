# User Manual

For members of the public, registered citizens, human rights officers and supervisors.
Administrators and awareness officers should read [ADMIN-GUIDE.md](ADMIN-GUIDE.md).

---

# Part 1 — For the public

## Filing a report

You do **not** need an account, and you do **not** have to give your name.

1. Click **Report a violation** from any page.
2. **Step 1 — Who is reporting.** Either fill in your details, or switch on **Report
   anonymously**. With the switch on, no name, phone number, email or address is stored.
3. **Step 2 — The incident.** A short title, the category, and a description of at least 30
   characters are required. Everything else helps but can be left blank. Set the urgency
   honestly — it determines how quickly the case must be actioned:

   | Urgency | First action due within |
   |---|---|
   | Critical (immediate risk) | 3 days |
   | High | 7 days |
   | Medium | 14 days |
   | Low | 30 days |

4. **Step 3 — Evidence.** Drag files in or click to browse. Up to 10 files, 20 MB each:
   images, video, audio, PDF and Word documents. A report with no attachments is still
   investigated — never put yourself at risk to gather evidence.
5. **Step 4 — Review and submit.** Check the summary, tick the consent declaration, submit.

You then receive a **reference number** like `HR-2026-000123`. Write it down. If you filed
anonymously, this number cannot be recovered — it is the only way to follow your report.

Click **Download acknowledgement** for a printable receipt (your browser's print dialogue can
save it as a PDF).

### If someone is in immediate danger

Call the hotline shown in the site footer first, then file the report and mark it **Critical**.

## Tracking a report

**Track report** → enter your reference number. You see a progress bar, the current status, and
the key dates. For your protection, tracking never shows case notes, evidence or the officer's
identity.

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Submitted | Received; case file created |
| Under Review | A supervisor is confirming it falls within our mandate |
| Assigned | An officer has been allocated |
| Investigating | Active investigation |
| Awaiting Information | We need something more from you — check your email |
| Escalated | Referred for senior review |
| Resolved | A remedy has been achieved |
| Closed | The file is concluded |
| Rejected | Outside our mandate; you will have been told why and where to go instead |

## Registering an account

Optional, but it keeps all your reports in one place and puts updates in one feed.

**Register** → complete the form → open the verification link emailed to you → sign in.
Your password needs at least 10 characters including upper case, lower case, a number and a
symbol. The strength meter is guidance; the rules are enforced on submission.

# Part 2 — For registered citizens

## My reports

**My reports** lists everything you have filed with this account, newest first. Anonymous
reports do not appear here — by design, they are not linked to you.

Open a report to see what you submitted, the evidence you attached, and a timeline of updates
the case team has chosen to share with you.

## Responding to an information request

If a case shows **Awaiting Information**, an officer needs something from you. You will get an
email and an in-app notification explaining exactly what. Reply using **Contact the case team**
on the report page, or the contact form, always quoting your reference number.

## Your profile and security

**My profile** — keep your email and phone current; that is how we reach you about your cases.

The same page shows **recent sign-in activity**: time, IP address and outcome for your last ten
attempts. If you see a successful sign-in you do not recognise, change your password
immediately and contact the administrator.

To change your password you must enter the current one first.

## Notifications

The bell in the top bar shows unread notifications. Click **Mark all read** to clear them, or
open **Notifications** for the full history.

# Part 3 — For human rights officers

## Your dashboard

Sign in and you land on the officer dashboard:

- **KPI tiles** — active cases, overdue, escalated, concluded, and your average resolution
  time. Each tile is a link into a filtered case list.
- **Charts** — twelve-month report volume, breakdown by category and by status.
- **Needs attention now** — cases past their deadline, oldest first. Start here.
- **Upcoming interviews** — everything you have scheduled, next first.
- **My open cases** — click any row to open the case.

## Working a case

Open a case from your list. The page has three parts: the complaint on the left, assignment
and complainant details on the right, and the investigation timeline at the bottom.

### Record an action

Use the tabs under **Record an action**:

| Tab | Use it to |
|---|---|
| **Case note** | Record what you did, found or decided. Tick *Visible to the complainant* to share it with them; leave it clear for an internal note. |
| **Upload findings** | Attach documents, photographs, recordings or reports you have gathered. |
| **Schedule interview** | Book an interview. It appears on your dashboard calendar and, being shared, on the complainant's timeline. |
| **Request info** | Ask the complainant for something specific. This emails them and sets the case to *Awaiting Information*. |
| **Change status** | Move the case through the investigative states, or escalate it. |

Write notes as though someone else will pick the case up tomorrow — because they might. The
timeline is the case file.

### What you cannot do

You cannot mark your own case **Resolved**, **Closed** or **Rejected** — that requires a
supervisor. If you believe a case is finished, add a note setting out your findings and
recommendation, then tell your supervisor.

You cannot open a case that is not assigned to you. Attempting it is refused and logged.

### Escalating

Use **Escalate** when a case needs authority or resources you do not have, when it involves a
senior official, or when it has stalled through no fault of the investigation. Always give a
reason in the note field. Supervisors and administrators are notified immediately.

### Evidence

Files appear in the **Evidence** panel — both what the complainant attached and what you have
added. Every time you open a file it is recorded in the audit log with your name. That is
normal and expected; it exists to protect complainants.

### Anonymous reports

If a report was filed anonymously the complainant panel says so, and there is no way to
contact them. Information requests will be recorded but cannot be delivered. Work from the
evidence and witnesses on file.

# Part 4 — For supervisors

## Assigning cases

New cases arrive under **Unassigned** in the sidebar (also a tile on your dashboard).

Open a case and use the **Assignment** panel. The officer dropdown shows each officer's current
open-case count, so you can balance workload as you assign. Add an instruction in the note
field — it goes into the timeline and the officer's notification.

Reassignment works the same way. The previous assignment is retained in the history rather than
overwritten, so the chain of custody stays intact.

## Monitoring

**Officer performance** gives you, per officer: total cases, open, concluded, overdue, average
days to resolve, and a completion bar. Use it for workload balance and supervision
conversations, not as a standalone performance score — case complexity varies enormously.

Watch two figures in particular:

- **Overdue** — anything above zero needs a conversation this week.
- **Average days** — a number drifting upward usually means workload, not capability.

## Concluding a case

Open the case → **Conclude the case** → choose the outcome:

| Outcome | When |
|---|---|
| **Resolved** | A remedy was achieved — reinstatement, release, access granted, compensation. |
| **Closed** | No further action is possible, e.g. the complainant withdrew or cannot be traced. |
| **Rejected** | The complaint falls outside our mandate. Say where the complainant should go instead. |

The resolution summary is **shared with the complainant**, so write it for them: what was
found, what was done, and what happens next. It requires at least 20 characters — but a
one-line conclusion on a six-month investigation is not adequate.

## Reports and exports

**Reports & analytics** gives daily, weekly, monthly, annual or custom-range figures, with
category, province and status breakdowns plus the officer table. Export as:

- **CSV** for spreadsheets and further analysis
- **Excel** for immediate opening in Excel or LibreOffice
- **PDF** — opens a print-ready page; use your browser's *Save as PDF*

Exports contain case-management data. Handle them under your organisation's information
security policy, and note that every export is recorded in the audit log with your name, the
period and the row count.
